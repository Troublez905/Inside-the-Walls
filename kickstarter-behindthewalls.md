# Behind the Walls - Kickstarter Campaign Draft

## Campaign Title

**Behind the Walls: Help Build Inside the Walls**

## Short Pitch

**Inside the Walls** is a third-person persistent online prison simulator for Windows PC where nobody is simply a hero, nobody is simply a villain, and every choice leaves a record.

Players enter a fictional North American correctional institution as either a newly arrived inmate or a probationary correctional officer. The prison keeps moving whether the server is full or quiet: AI inmates, officers, and staff fill the empty roles, follow routines, remember events, and react to what players do. Meals, counts, jobs, yard time, reports, relationships, discipline, medical care, education, and lockdowns are not background flavor. They are the game.

The promise is simple:

**Nobody leaves unchanged.**

## The Big Idea

Most prison games focus on escape, punishment, or chaos. **Inside the Walls** is about pressure, procedure, reputation, and the uneasy humanity of people forced to share the same system.

As an inmate, you manage your day, your reputation, your work, your debts, your programs, your friendships, and the small choices that decide whether tomorrow gets easier or worse.

As an officer, you balance safety, procedure, discretion, reports, staffing pressure, and the difference between control and leadership. A rushed decision can calm a unit, damage trust, or become an incident that follows your career.

This is a game about routine becoming drama. A missed count matters. A late meal movement matters. A favor remembered by the wrong person matters. A report written carefully can change someone else's future. A report written badly can change yours.

## Why Kickstarter

We are building a detailed simulation, not a quick arena game with prison wallpaper. That means the core systems need time, testing, art, animation, networking, and careful design.

Kickstarter funding lets us move from a promising foundation into a playable public path:

- Finish the offline prototype until one complete simplified prison day works end-to-end.
- Build the first low-security facility with playable inmate and officer routes.
- Produce the character, environment, UI, and animation assets needed for a strong vertical slice.
- Prove dedicated-server multiplayer before expanding toward larger player counts.
- Bring backers into the process through progress builds, behind-the-scenes updates, and controlled early testing.

## What Makes It Different

- **Two playable sides of the same day:** inmate life and officer work are connected, not separate modes.
- **A living institution:** AI fills empty roles so the prison keeps functioning below full player count.
- **Consequences over chaos:** violence, rule-breaking, discretion, trust, and reporting all create follow-up effects.
- **Persistent identity:** relationships, work history, conduct, assignments, reputation, rank, and records matter across sessions.
- **Grounded stylized realism:** readable 3D art, believable facilities, restrained tension, and no grimdark shock value.
- **Respectful character systems:** background, culture, religion, ethnicity, and appearance never determine morality, aggression, or faction membership.
- **Procedure as gameplay:** schedules, access, permissions, reports, keys, posts, programs, and official counts drive the pressure.

## The Game We Want To Build

### Play As An Inmate

You arrive with a name, a history, a housing assignment, and not much control over your day. You learn the schedule, watch the room, choose who to trust, decide whether to keep promises, take jobs, attend programs, avoid trouble, earn privileges, build influence, or prepare for transfer and reentry.

Progress is not a single level bar. You build separate reputations with staff, housing units, work details, programs, and other people. You can become reliable, feared, useful, respected, ignored, watched, trusted, or marked by past decisions.

### Play As An Officer

You start as a probationary correctional officer. You are not a superhero in a uniform. You are new, watched, tested, and expected to make decisions under pressure.

Your day includes briefings, posts, movement, counts, doors, observations, reports, searches, communication, de-escalation, incident review, and consequences. Promotions come from judgment, documentation, training, attendance, communication, and safe decisions. Abuse, negligence, corruption, unnecessary force, and falsified reports can trigger investigations and career damage.

### A Prison That Keeps Moving

Inside the Walls is designed around a living schedule:

- Wake-up
- Meals
- Medication and medical call-outs
- Work
- School
- Official counts
- Programs
- Appointments
- Recreation
- Visitation
- Evening unit time
- Final count
- Lockdown

The prison can react to shortages, searches, emergencies, maintenance issues, overcrowding, transfers, and incidents. AI characters have roles, routines, posts, perception, memory, and needs so the facility does not feel frozen when human players are offline.

## Campaign Tone

The writing should feel tense, grounded, and human. Avoid making the prison feel like a haunted house, a power fantasy, or a stereotype machine.

Use phrases like:

- Pressure through procedure.
- Every choice leaves a record.
- The schedule does not care how your day is going.
- The prison remembers what people saw.
- Routine is the engine. Consequence is the hook.
- Nobody leaves unchanged.

Avoid phrases like:

- Ultimate chaos.
- Become the king of prison.
- Brutal jail warfare.
- Good cops versus bad inmates.
- Real prison tactics.
- Anything that sounds like instructions for wrongdoing.

## Risks And Honesty

Inside the Walls is ambitious. Persistent multiplayer simulation, AI routines, role-based gameplay, and consequence systems are all expensive to build correctly.

The responsible path is staged:

1. Finish the offline day.
2. Verify the role loops.
3. Replace gray boxes with focused production art.
4. Prove two-client multiplayer.
5. Add living-prison AI.
6. Test with 8-12 players.
7. Scale only after the smaller version is stable.

Backers should support the project because they want to see this kind of careful simulation exist, and because the campaign is honest about the road between concept, prototype, alpha, beta, and release.
